YouTube Shorts · with Google DeepMind

Edit with AI

Taking generative video creation from a creator problem to launch.

1 · Problem

Many creators, especially new ones, struggled to get started. Facing a blank editor, they often didn't create at all. I led the discussion on how to lower that barrier through inspiration, ease of use, or both.

2 · Decision

We chose to redesign the creation flow around generative AI. The key call was the model: an outside tool would have integrated faster, but brought legal and privacy complexity. I led the build-versus-partner analysis, and we chose to build on Gemini with Google DeepMind, accepting a slower start.

Research with creators set the scope: understand the footage, assemble it into a coherent story, and add the finishing touches creators spend the most time on, like music, voiceover, and transitions.

3 · Execution

I wrote the product requirements and built the user flows with design. With research engineers, I defined what good editing looks like across common video styles, which shaped how the model was tuned.

  • Latency over peak quality. We chose a faster model over a higher-quality one and designed an asynchronous flow so creators never waited on generation.
  • Safety built in from the start. We tuned safety checks to catch harmful content reliably without slowing uploads, and set strict limits on how long user data is kept.
  • Speed over flexibility for voiceover. We pre-generated a small set of fine-tuned voices instead of generating them live, to keep the experience fast.
  • Inspiration that fits each region. I led an operations workstream to surface example videos and music that were relevant, fresh, and remixable in each market.
4 · Evidence

Side-by-side evaluations compared the model's edits against creator-made ones on coherence and quality, and we iterated on the gaps before launch. Music synced to the beat also improved creation beyond the AI flow.

Launched to creators and increased Shorts creation.

5 · Learning

Expanding AI voiceover to new languages showed that quality doesn't transfer automatically. Each language needs its own evaluation, not a copy of the English one.

Google · Store, Fi, and Pixel

Ask Google Help

Rebuilding device troubleshooting around one AI agent, and defining how agents act for users.

1 · Problem

Troubleshooting help was spread across several separate systems, each handling part of the journey. At the same time, agents were gaining the ability to take actions, with no shared rule for when an agent may act on a user's behalf.

2 · Decision

We consolidated everything into one orchestration layer that sends troubleshooting to Ask Google Help. I also defined the agentic actions framework, now applied across Google products for support cases: when an agent acts autonomously, when a human stays in the loop, and when it needs the user's consent.

3 · Execution

I defined how response quality is judged, so every answer is clear, accurate, and leads to a solution. We kept improving quality to resolve more issues without handing off to a human agent.

I also defined how sensitive issues, such as legal, safety, or privacy concerns, are identified and routed to people.

4 · Evidence

Higher customer satisfaction, more troubleshooting handled by the agent, and better retention among users switching to Pixel.

5 · Learning

Deciding when an agent should hand off is a trade-off between protecting support teams from false alarms and making sure sensitive issues are never missed. We started conservative and keep tuning that balance.
